Blended Learning Programme Design

Psychology + Cybersecurity CPD Course

Programme Design CPD Accredited Blended Learning 30 Learners

The Brief

MindHug, a mental health professional development company, had won an Innovate UK government contract to train 30 specialist coaches in the intersection of cybersecurity and psychology of change. The idea: coaches already skilled in supporting clients through emotional difficulty could extend that practice to cybersecurity incidents — a context their clients were increasingly facing.

The problem was that most of these coaches described themselves as non-technical. Cybersecurity wasn't just unfamiliar — for many, it carried a sense of this isn't for people like me.

The programme needed CPD accreditation and had to run within a three-month contract window.

My Role

I was responsible for the learning architecture and the design of interactive experiences. That meant:

  • Aligning with cybersecurity and psychology subject matter experts on what the programme needed to achieve
  • Defining the learning objectives and identifying which behaviours we actually needed to shift
  • Designing the activities that would do the work — the interactions, scenarios, and vocabulary tasks where learning would happen

The SMEs provided the content expertise and led most of the live facilitation. My job was to create the structure that made their knowledge teachable, and the interventions that made it stick.

Impact & Outcomes

30 Coaches Trained
CPD Accredited

Knowledge & Confidence

All participants demonstrated knowledge improvement on post-programme assessments and reported increased confidence in supporting clients through cybersecurity incidents.

Behavior Change & Application

Follow-up conversations revealed tangible changes in how participants approached their work:

  • One participant who previously viewed compliance as "such a chore" became an advocate, eventually speaking publicly about cybersecurity at professional events
  • Several participants (5 of 30) shifted their self-perception from "I'm not a technical person" to "I can communicate in this language"—enabling them to engage more confidently with technical professionals
  • Participants educated peers about cyber threats in their own organizations, translating technical concepts for non-technical colleagues
  • Multiple participants pursued further specialized training in cybersecurity topics, indicating sustained interest beyond the programme

Key Insight

The most significant outcome wasn't just knowledge gain—it was identity shift. Participants who previously avoided technical contexts began seeing themselves as capable of navigating that world, which opened new professional possibilities.
